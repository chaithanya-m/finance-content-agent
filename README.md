## Topic: Why Interest Rates Affect Home Loan EMIs in India

## Key Facts

- **Central Bank Policy Rate**: The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) sets the benchmark policy rate, which influences overall lending rates in the economy.
- **Loan Interest Rates**: Most home loans in India are linked to the RBI policy rate through market-based refinancing rates (like MSFR - Market Sensitive Fixed Rate).
- **EMI Calculation**: The Equated Monthly Installment (EMI) is calculated as:
  **EMI = P × r × (1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n - 1)**
  where *P* = principal loan amount, *r* = monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12), and *n* = total number of months.
- **Interest Rate Impact**: When the RBI policy rate rises, the MSFR increases, leading to higher monthly interest payments and thus higher EMIs. Conversely, when the policy rate falls, EMIs decrease.
- **Regulatory Framework**: The RBI regulates home loan interest rates through various schemes (e.g., NCLR - National Capital Region Loan Scheme, and various state-specific home loan regulations) to ensure borrower protection and financial stability.

## Important Numbers

- RBI policy rate typically ranges between 4% and 6.5% (as of 2026)
- Typical home loan tenure: 15-30 years
- Average home loan amounts in India: ₹50 lakh to ₹50 crore
- EMI sensitivity: A 1% increase in the policy rate can lead to approximately 1-2% increase in monthly EMI for similar loan terms.

## Context

In India, most home loans are offered by banks and NBFCs (Non-Banking Financial Companies) that follow market-linked interest rates. The RBI's monetary policy decisions directly impact the cost of borrowing for home buyers. When the RBI hikes rates to combat inflation, housing affordability decreases as EMIs rise. When the RBI cuts rates to stimulate growth, home loan EMIs become more affordable.

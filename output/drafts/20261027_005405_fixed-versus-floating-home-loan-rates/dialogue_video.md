## Full Teaching Dialogue

*(Use this to understand the conversational flow before rendering scenes.)*

**🧑 Arjun [HOOK]:** Arjun: Hey, Riya! I'm thinking about buying a house, and I'm a bit confused about fixed versus floating home loan rates. Can you explain this to me?

**👩 Riya [SCENARIO]:** Riya: Sure, Arjun! Imagine you have a piggy bank where you put your allowance. Now, if you put your money in a piggy bank that doesn't change, that's like a fixed-rate home loan. But if you put your money in a piggy bank that can change, like a bank account, that's like a floating-rate home loan.

**👩 Riya [EXPLANATION]:** Riya: With a fixed-rate home loan, the interest rate stays the same throughout the loan period. This means you'll pay the same amount each month, which can be easier to budget for. But if interest rates go up, you might have to pay more money than you planned.

**👩 Riya [EXAMPLE]:** Riya: Let's say you take a fixed-rate home loan of Rs. 50 lakh at an annual interest rate of 8%. If the interest rate goes up to 9% after 5 years, you'll have to pay more money each month to keep up with the higher interest. That's why it's important to check your repayment capacity and understand how floating-rate loans work.

**👩 Riya [CONSEQUENCES]:** Riya: If you don't understand floating-rate home loans, you might end up paying more money than you need to, or you might not be able to afford the loan at all. It's like if you put your money in a piggy bank that can change, and then the piggy bank keeps changing to a higher amount. You might not have enough money to buy the house you want.

**👩 Riya [TAKEAWAY]:** Riya: To avoid these problems, it's a good idea to talk to a financial advisor or a bank representative who can explain the different types of home loans and help you choose the best one for your needs.

## Verified facts (do not change or add to these)

- UCBs must check borrowers' repayment capacity for floating-rate housing loans, including possible EMI increases when benchmark rates rise.
- UCBs are advised to have a policy for resetting floating rates on EMI-based housing loans.
